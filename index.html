01

One word, two nets

In Arabic, شبكة (shabaka) is a fishing net and it is a phone or internet network. In Gaza, when the signal goes, people say there is no shabaka. The keffiyeh carries the fisherman's net in its weave. Palestine Online is built from that one word.

شبكة

The net
Threads crossed and knotted at every crossing. It holds because every knot holds its neighbours. Torn, it lets things through.
The network
Addresses joined by routes. It holds while the routes are carried. Cut, a place drops off the map of the internet.

02

The mark

A hash sign is how a voice is tagged online. Turn it forty-five degrees and it is a cell of the net: two threads each way, four knots, eight cut ends. The knot at the top is the one that answers.

Geometry

Drawn on a 100-unit square before the turn. Threads sit 17 units either side of the centre and run 11 units past the knots. A thread is 5.6 units; a knot, 10.4. The cut ends are the mark's edge: its clear space is one cut end on every side.

Sizes

The mark alone holds down to 16 pixels; the favicon is drawn at that size. With the name, keep the mark at least 24 pixels tall.

The lit knot

Only the top knot changes colour, and only to sea blue. Everything else is thread: ink on cotton, cotton on night.

03

Lockups

The name is set in Schibsted Grotesk at weight 780 and outlined, with the line beneath in Azeret Mono. The mark is twice the cap height and centred on it.

  • Don't turn it backUpright, it is only a hash sign.
  • Don't light every knotOne knot answers. That is the point.
  • Don't stretch itThe cell is square before it is turned.
  • Don't colour it by statusThe mark is not a status. The board has its own glyphs.

06

States of the net

The board never uses a generic dot. Every state is drawn from the net: a cell that holds, sags, tears or is mended; a single knot for one event; a run of knots for a pattern; a loose end for an open case; two threads paused for a stalled one.

  • DownBelow a tenth of its level before the war.
  • DegradedMeasurably below its pre-war level.
  • SteadyNo change worth a headline in 30 days.
  • RestoredBack after an outage we reported.
  • IncidentOne event, with a place and a date.
  • PatternA trend a named monitor documents.
  • OpenA case still unresolved.
  • StalledA case with no progress reported.
  • ResolvedA case that ended: a release, a ruling.
  • RecognisedWork that won a prize or a hearing.

07

The net as a chart

The lead of the board is drawn as the net itself. One knot for every address block Gaza had before the war: 704. Today 155 hold and 8 answer. The rest are drawn as the ghost of the net that was.

  • One knot, one block.A block is 256 addresses. Never round, never scale.
  • Order, not a map.Knots are laid from the headrope down. IODA does not place blocks on a map, and neither do we.
  • Ghost, not gone.Blocks that are no longer routed stay drawn in dashed thread, so the loss keeps its size.
  • Lit means answering.Only knots with a device that replies to IODA's probes are sea blue.

08

Motion

Motion shows what the data does: cast, tear, mend, ping. Nothing moves for decoration, and all of it stops for reduced motion.

  • CastThe first visit: a hash sign turns into a cell, its knots are tied, the net spreads from it across the screen, the cell flies to the masthead. 2.7 seconds, once a session, skipped for reduced motion.Watch it →
  • TearThe lead net first appears whole, as it was before the war. Then the blocks that are no longer routed fall away, row by row, into ghost.
  • MendScrub the month and the net knits and tears to each day's count: 147 in late September, 155 since 9 October.
  • PingEvery second and a half, one answering knot sends a ring, the way a device answers a probe.
  • TieHover the name and the top knot tightens.

10

Photographs

Photographs show the person, the place or the thing in the entry, in their own colour, captioned with where and when they were taken. Never a picture of something else. When there is no honest photograph, the entry carries its state on night instead.
